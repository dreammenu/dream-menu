This quarter

Three findings, and what to do about them

Tested against chance, not just counted. Figures are shares of answers from 1,256 interviews to 10 November 2026. The menus card sets those answers beside 45 published menus read on 13 November 2026. The fourth card is an outside source, kept separate as a challenge to our own data.

1
Movement

Sides are where the menu is moving

18.3% of side answers now sit in a different food group than in late 2025. Sampling alone would move about 8.0%. Starters, mains and desserts are within chance.

What to doReview the side list first. Change starters, mains and desserts for cost or quality, not to chase a trend.

What changed, course by course →
2
Menus

Tiramisu: 12.2% of guests, 23% of menus

We read the published menus of 45 of the venues guests name most. Tiramisu is the most-named dessert, yet it is on 23% of the 30 menus that print desserts. Other chocolate desserts are on 57%, for 2.7% of guests.

What to doCheck your dessert list against the 3 most-named (tiramisu, sticky toffee pudding, baked cheesecake) before adding anything new.

What guests ask for against what menus list →
3
Drinks

Cocktails lead; 7.9% choose alcohol-free

Cocktails take 41.1% of alcoholic drink answers. 7.9% answer the question with something alcohol-free. An early baseline from 151 answers since August 2026.

What to doGive alcohol-free its own priced section, not a footnote, and lead the drinks list with house cocktails.

The new drinks series →
Outside check

Matcha: growth and backlash at once

The trend guests most want binned, named by 9.2% of all interviews. Tastewise, UK consumer and menu data (June 2026) reports the opposite: consumers choosing matcha lattes 33.5% more than a year earlier; matcha dishes on UK menus doubled.

What to doServe it well for the fans who want it, but don’t build a menu identity around it.
