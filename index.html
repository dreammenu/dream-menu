This quarter

Three findings and an outside check

Tested against chance, not just counted. Figures are shares of answers from 1,340 interviews to 23 November 2026. The menus card sets those answers beside 45 published menus read on 13 November 2026. The grey card is an outside source, kept separate as a challenge to our own data. Each card ends with what to do about it.

1
Movement

Sides are where the menu is moving

Sides are changing more than twice as fast as chance would explain: 18.1% of side answers have moved to a different food group since late 2025, against about 7.9% you would expect from the luck of who was asked. Starters, mains and desserts have moved no more than chance would explain.

What to doReview the side list first. Change starters, mains and desserts for cost or quality, not to chase a trend.

What changed, course by course →
2
Menus

Tiramisu: 12.2% of guests, 23.3% of menus

We read the published menus of 45 of the venues guests name most. Tiramisu is the most-named dessert, yet it is on 23.3% of the 30 menus that print desserts. Other chocolate desserts are on 56.7%, for 2.7% of guests.

What to doCheck your dessert list against the 3 most-named (tiramisu, sticky toffee pudding, baked cheesecake) before adding anything new.

What guests ask for against what menus list →
3
Drinks

Cocktails lead; 7.5% choose alcohol-free

Cocktails take 44.3% of alcoholic drink answers. 7.5% answer the question with something alcohol-free. An early baseline from 174 answers since August 2026.

What to doGive alcohol-free its own priced section, not a footnote, and lead the drinks list with house cocktails.

Outside check

Matcha: growth and backlash at once

The trend guests most want binned, named by 9.2% of all interviews. Tastewise, UK consumer and menu data (June 2026) reports the opposite: consumers choosing matcha lattes 33.5% more than a year earlier; matcha dishes on UK menus doubled.

What to doServe it well for the fans who want it, but don’t build a menu identity around it.

The dishes people choose

For each course, the share of guests whose dream dish is that specific dish. Scallops lead the starters, tiramisu and sticky toffee pudding the desserts, and skinny fries only just beat triple-cooked chips. Named this precisely, no single dish takes more than one answer in eight.

iHow to read this

Each card is one course. The percentage is the share of that course’s answers naming the dish; every bar on every card uses the same scale, so a longer bar is a bigger share whichever course it is in.

“Steak” means guests who named steak without a cut; fillet steak is counted on its own. Answers that fit no dish are left out, so shares are of answers we could code.
