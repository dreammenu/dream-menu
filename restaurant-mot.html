An independent check of one venue

The report tells you what Glasgow is eating. The MOT tells you what your community thinks of you, how you compare with the benchmarks, and where your most valuable improvements are. It is not a review and not consultancy: it is the outside view most operators never get.

How it works

  1. Week 1

    The listen

    15 to 25 structured interviews with food-literate diners who have visited in the last six months.

  2. Week 2

    The look

    Two unannounced visits as a paying guest: one at peak, one quieter.

  3. Week 3

    The numbers

    A unit economics conversation. No full P&L needed; what you are comfortable sharing.

  4. Week 4

    The report

    A written report with scored sections and recommendations ranked by impact and cost, plus a one-hour debrief.

Recommendations come in three groups: quick wins for this week, medium investments with clear returns, and strategic decisions only you can make.

Who it is for

  • A quality independent not getting the advocacy its food deserves. A brand or communication gap: the food earns five stars, the first impressions earn three.
  • A new opening approaching its first anniversary. A positioning gap, or being used for a different occasion than the one you designed for.
  • An established venue that has changed and lost advocacy. A trust gap: the people who loved the original need a reason to reconnect.
  • A venue considering a big change. A clear map of what belongs to the community and what belongs to the kitchen.
  • A group wanting one framework across sites. Variance between sites that is hard to see from inside.

What it is not

Not a rescue package, not implementation support and not a guarantee. It is independent: Dream Menu has no financial relationship with any Glasgow venue and no advertising revenue. The operators who get most from it read it as a challenge, not a verdict.

The most valuable thing an outside view can give you is not the answer. It's the question you didn't know you weren't asking.
