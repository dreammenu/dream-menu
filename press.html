Dream Menu asks chefs, creators and foodies one question: build your dream menu, one dish per course, from places you actually go. 1,340 interviews later, these are the figures most worth quoting, how to credit them and who to call.

What the evidence says for you

Marketing, press and guides

40% of the 25 most-named venues hold a current guide award (84% counting trade awards and other lists), against 7% of all venues named (12%): guests and critics agree at the very top and part ways below it. The favourites beyond the guides are the stories nobody else has. A fresh line from the menu audit: tiramisu is the most-named dessert, at 12.2% of answers, yet 77% of the dessert menus we read leave it off.

Checked against outside research

Published research backs Dream Menu on 13 of 21 topics; 6 more are local insights. Each finding below is backed by a published source; quote both with their dates.

  • Pineapple on pizza. 49.6% say yes. Backed by YouGov, GB adults (2017): 53% of Britons like pineapple on pizza. Source
  • Tap water with dinner. 34.7% ask for tap water. Backed by Local Government Association survey, 3,000 people (2016): About a third of people drink tap water when eating out in restaurants. Source
  • Tiramisu. Steady staple: 12.2% of dessert answers, between 10.3% and 14.5% in every full quarter. Backed by Tastewise, UK consumer data (June 2026): Tiramisu up nearly 15% in consumer demand. Source
  • Italian food. Italian ranks on starters, mains and desserts: 8.0% of starters, 8.6% of mains, and tiramisu is the most-named dessert (12.2%). Backed by YouGov, GB adults (April 2022): Italian is Britain’s favourite cuisine, chosen by 51% (Chinese 46%, British 43%, Indian 41%). Source
  • Sticky toffee pudding. The number 2 dessert, 11.1% of dessert answers. Backed by YouGov, classic British dishes ranked (June 2019): 73% of Britons who have tried sticky toffee pudding like it, one of the best-liked British desserts. Source
  • Takeaway pizza. The most-named guilty pleasure, named by 6.6% of all interviews. Backed by YouGov, GB adults (April 2022): Takeaway pizza is one of Britain’s three favourite junk foods, picked by 31% (41 to 42% of under-40s). Source
  • Diet and zero-sugar cola. Diet Coke and Coke Zero are named by 10.2% of soft drink answers, against 3.6% for full-sugar Coca-Cola. Backed by British Soft Drinks Association, Annual Industry Insights Report (July 2026): More than seven in ten soft drinks sold in the UK are low- or no-calorie. Source
  • Margaritas. Margarita and spicy margarita together take 10.7% of alcoholic drink answers, the biggest single cocktail family. Backed by Bacardi Cocktail Trends Report, via Morning Advertiser (December 2025): The Margarita is number one in the top 10 cocktails for 2026. Source
  • Martini. 5.3% of alcoholic drink answers, the number 3 cocktail. Backed by Bacardi Cocktail Trends Report, via Morning Advertiser (December 2025): The Dry Martini is in the top 10 cocktails for 2026. Source
  • Alcohol-free. 8.1% answer the alcoholic drink question with something alcohol-free. Backed by Drinkaware Monitor (October 2025): 44% of UK adults choose no- or low-alcohol drinks to moderate (49% of young adults), up from 31% in 2018. Source
  • Word of mouth. The 10% most-named venues take 44% of all venue mentions. Backed by YouGov, Gen Z eating out (August 2026): 59% of Gen Z discover restaurants through recommendations from friends or family; 43% through social media. Source
  • Small plates. One of the trends guests most want binned, named by 3.0% of all interviews. Backed by OpenTable, reported by The Independent (March 2026): 56% of UK diners now prefer individual dishes to sharing plates. Source
  • Scottish seafood. Seafood is 35.7% of dream starters; scallops are the most-named starter (7.7%). Backed by Seafood Scotland, Making Waves (April 2025): Scallops are the most favoured Scottish seafood species in UK Michelin-starred restaurants; 80% of starred restaurants use Scottish seafood. The method in brief

Guests answer the same questions through Instagram: a starter, main, side, dessert and drinks, and the venue that does each best, plus a hidden gem and four debates. Answers are coded by hand to 348 dish categories. Venues are matched and placed on the map (1,529 of 1,740 pinned). It is qualitative fieldwork with a large base, not a statistically representative survey: guests are weighted towards Glasgow and towards people who work in or write about food. Movement over time is only called when it passes a two-proportion test. Full method.
