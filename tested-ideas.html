At a glance

The evidence map

Every idea placed by how much difference it could make and how well the research backs it. Top right is where to start. Tap a dot to jump to the idea.

    How to read it. Impact: how much it could move profit per cover or how guests feel. Evidence: 5 is a controlled study in real restaurants or hotels, 1 is theory or a vendor's claim. Cost, ease and risk are in the league table.
    Infographic

    How big is the effect?

    The headline result from the best study behind each idea. Darker bars mean stronger evidence. A big number from a small study is a reason to test, not a promise.

    Effects are relative changes in what each study measured (spend, orders, attendance, waste). Ranges show the low and high estimates where a study gave both. Ideas with no measured effect in a comparable setting are left out.

    Scorecard

    The league table

    Each score out of 100 is built from five parts. The bar shows where an idea earns its points, so a cheap, easy idea with modest evidence can be told apart from a proven but costly one.

        Weights: impact 30, evidence 25, cost 15, ease 15, risk 15. Score 80 and over: use now. 70 to 79: use with conditions. 60 to 69: pilot first. Under 60: hold. One idea (the bigger wine glass) is held back on risk despite its score.

        Do it properly

        Run your own trial

        Every idea above comes with a test. The method is the same each time: change one thing, run old and new in turns, count the same number for both.

        1. Week 1Old way
        2. Week 2New way
        3. Week 3Old way
        4. Week 4New way
        5. ThenCompare the totals

        Alternating evens out paydays, weather, events and staff rotas, which would swamp a simple before-and-after.

        Trial planner: how long do I need?

        Small changes need lots of tables to show up reliably. Put in your own numbers.

        Standard settings: 95% confidence, 80% power, spend per table varying by about half its average. A guide, not a guarantee.

        Why pooling venues matters

        Weeks needed to prove each effect, at a typical 60-cover independent (160 tables and 100 bookings a week) on its own, against five venues running the same trial.

        Dream Menu Trials. We're looking for five to ten venues to run one idea a quarter together, sharing weekly till totals only (no guest data). Results come back to you first, then onto this page. Put your venue forward.
        How the ideas were scored
        PartWeightA 5 meansA 1 means
        Impact30Likely to move profit per cover or guest experience noticeablyMarginal, or only in narrow cases
        Evidence25Controlled field study in restaurants or hotels, ideally repeatedTheory, one lab study or a vendor's claim
        Cost15Close to freeNeeds capital spend
        Ease15Done in an afternoon by the current teamBuilding work, training or new systems
        Risk15Little chance of backfiringReal ethical, legal or reputational exposure

        This is a desk test. Most studies come from one venue or one country, some evidence is borrowed from nearby settings (healthcare, car washes, university dining halls), and published effects often shrink when repeated. That is why every idea ends with a trial.
