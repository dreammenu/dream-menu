What the evidence says for you

Supplying venues or building a range

Build the core range around the 16 steady staples and follow the shift within sides. Partners see which food groups gained and lost, a score for every dish and a three-quarter planning range. The menu audit shows where listings trail demand: tiramisu (12.1% of guests, 23% of menus); sticky toffee pudding (11.0% of guests, 23% of menus); cheesecake (6.3% of guests, 23% of menus).

Partner briefing

6Category scorecardPartners

4 categories to grow, 6 to defend or trim

The buying view. Every food group in every course, with its share of demand, how that share has moved, and a call: Grow, Defend, Trim, Core or Niche. A move only earns a call when it passes a significance test.

Use it torun a range review. Start with Grow and Defend: those are the lines where a decision is due.

7Where to sell itPartners

The venues to call, range by range

From categories to accounts. Every Scottish venue guests name at least twice, scored for each supplier range by the dishes guests name there and whether those dishes are worth backing. Each row gives the dish to lead with and anything falling at that venue. The full rep sheet adds every venue, addresses, your own customer marks and print.

Use it toplan a call list. Pick a range and a market, then work down the list: each venue comes with the dish to lead with and what is falling there.

8What to backPartners

A score for every dish and drink

From categories to single dishes. One ranking weighs size, momentum, strength of evidence and how open the field is: Back, Watch or Review. The matrix shows why a dish scores as it does.

Use it tostart range conversations from the Back list, then use the matrix to see whether a dish is big, growing or both.

9How sure, and where nextPartners

Which moves are real, and how far they go

Use it toseparate signal from noise, then plan stock from the shaded range, not the single line.

10Who buysPartners

How guests differ, and how operators feel

Plan the range for the customer you sell to: trade, creators and enthusiasts answer differently. Then the people who buy: what operators feel, and where their response falls short of the pressure.

Use it tomatch the range to the customer, and lead sales conversations with the pressures operators name most.
