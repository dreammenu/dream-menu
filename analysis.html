What should I do?

The same evidence, read for four kinds of reader

Each recommendation follows from the sections above and is rewritten when the numbers change.

Running a venue or a kitchen

Review the side list first: it is the only course that has moved beyond chance. Protect the staples (tiramisu, prawns and langoustines and triple-cooked chips). Give alcohol-free its own priced section: 7.5% of guests choose it when asked for an alcoholic drink. On the dessert list, check the 3 most-named desserts first (tiramisu, sticky toffee pudding, baked cheesecake): together they are 28.7% of dessert answers, and each is on 23% or fewer of the menus we read.

Operator toolkit →

Opening a venue or investing

Guests broadly agree on what desserts should be, so compete there on quality, not novelty. Starters are the most open course: that is where a signature dish can stand out. Recommendation is concentrated (the top 10% of venues take 44% of mentions), so plan for word of mouth from day one. For pricing, the middle printed price at the 45 audited venues is £9.00 for a starter and £17.95 for a main (half of all mains fall between £15.50 and £23.42).

Venue map and rankings →

Supplying venues or building a range

Build the core range around the 13 steady staples and follow the shift within sides. Partners see which food groups gained and lost, a score for every dish and a three-quarter planning range. The menu audit shows where listings trail demand: tiramisu (12.2% of guests, 23% of menus); sticky toffee pudding (11.1% of guests, 23% of menus); baked cheesecake (5.4% of guests, 23% of menus).

Marketing, press and guides

40% of the 25 most-named venues hold a current guide award (84% counting trade awards and other lists), against 7% of all venues named (12%): guests and critics agree at the very top and part ways below it. The favourites beyond the guides are the stories nobody else has. A fresh line from the menu audit: tiramisu is the most-named dessert, at 12.2% of answers, yet 77% of the dessert menus we read leave it off.
