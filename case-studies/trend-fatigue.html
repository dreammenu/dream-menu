Trend fatigue

Every interview asks which food trend should quietly disappear. Set against what guests actually choose, the answers show which trends are tiring and what is replacing them.

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

Guests are tired of food made to be posted, not of comfort: hype-led trends top the "bin it" list while chips give way to greens and mezze, and the honest classics hold.

  • 80%of top-10 "bin it"

    The fatigue is with the hype

    Matcha, Dubai chocolate and filming food lead, with viral TikTok food close behind.

  • −11.4pts

    Chips are giving way

    Potato sides fell from 45.1% to 33.7% of dream sides, a clear move.

  • 83mentions

    Comfort holds

    Takeaway pizza tops the guilty pleasures, with pasta and noodles close behind.

What we recommend

  1. Drop the viral special
  2. Make greens and salads as considered as the chips
  3. Add a Mediterranean or mezze-style side
  4. Keep one loaded item at most
  5. Explain small plates clearly

02 · What guests want gone

Guests are tired of food made for the camera

Unprompted answers to "Do you have a food trend you wish would quietly disappear?"

So what for a menu team

80% of the top ten "bin it" mentions are about hype: viral flavours, filming food and gimmick plating. The fatigue is with the performance, not with any one ingredient.

03 · What replaces it

The side order is moving from chips to greens

What guests actually choose, early against recent.

So what for a menu team

Loaded fries fell from 5.4% to 2.1% and potato sides lost 11.4 points overall. Greens, salads and mezze-style sides took the space.

04 · What stays

Comfort food is not going anywhere

The guilty pleasures guests admit to.

Loaded fries are stupid unless you've made them yourself at 3am.

Dream Menu guest, October 2026
So what for venues

Guests want honest comfort, not comfort dressed up for the camera. A well-made classic does not need a twist.

05 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Drop the viral special

    Put the effort into a dish people come back for. Hype-led trends dominate the "bin it" list.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNext menu change
  2. 02

    Make greens and salads as considered as the chips

    Vegetable and salad sides rose from 14.1% to 19.7%; greens and creamed spinach clearly up.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNext menu change
  3. 03

    Add a Mediterranean or mezze-style side

    From 4.0% to 9.3% of dream sides, a clear move.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowNext quarter
  4. 04

    Keep one loaded item at most

    Loaded fries fell from 5.4% to 2.1%; loaded and dirty food draws 38 "bin it" mentions.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowNow
  5. 05

    Explain small plates clearly

    Small plates appear in the "bin it" list (36). If that is the format, be clear on portions and price.

    Impact: LowEffort: LowNow

06 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

The next viral flavour

Hype cycles are short. A new entry climbing the "bin it" list within two quarters of peaking would confirm the pattern.

Chips recovery

Potato sides fell sharply. A winter rebound would suggest seasonality rather than fatigue.

Small plates

If small plates keep rising in the "bin it" list, it becomes a format risk, not just a pricing one.

Matcha

Matcha leads the list. Watch whether it fades from menus or settles as a drink rather than a flavour.

07 · Appendix

Method and sources

497"bin it" mentions (top 10)
1,218dream sides
455guilty pleasure mentions (top 10)
6quarters tracked

"Bin it" and guilty pleasure answers are unprompted and grouped into published labels; one answer can name more than one trend. Side shares use interviews with a codable side answer.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
