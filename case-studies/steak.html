The steak economy

Steak is the largest single idea on the main course. This report reads the cuts guests name, the quiet shift from fillet to flavour, and the plate around the meat.

Written for
A beef supplier or butcher selling to restaurants
The question
Which cuts and products should we push to restaurants next?
Status
Provisional sample, 29 October 2026

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

Steak is the biggest main course idea and still growing, but guests are moving from fillet to flavour cuts, braises and the steak pie, so the range should follow the flavour.

  • 25.5%of mains

    Beef leads the main course

    311 of 1,219 dream mains, rising from 21.9% to 27.2% (not yet a clear move).

  • +2.4pts

    The steak pie is back

    From 0.7% to 3.1% of mains: a clear move.

  • −1.0pts

    Fillet is slipping

    Short rib and ribeye rise as fillet falls: guests are trading to flavour.

What we recommend

  1. Lead the range with flavour cuts
  2. Supply a premium steak pie
  3. Package braises for small kitchens
  4. Sell the plate, not just the cut
  5. Target the venues guests already name

02 · The main course

A quarter of dream mains are steak or beef

Where beef sits against every other main course group, and the cuts guests name.

03 · The cuts

Guests are trading from fillet to flavour

Early against recent, and quarter by quarter.

So what for a beef supplier

Guests are trading towards flavour: short rib (2.3% to 4.5%), ribeye (1.6% to 3.4%) and steak pie (0.7% to 3.1%, a clear move) are rising while fillet slips (3.9% to 2.9%). Those are typically the cuts with the better margin per plate.

04 · The plate and the venues

Greens are the new steak side

The venues guests name for beef, and what goes beside it.

So what for venues

The steak plate is changing around the meat: greens and creamed spinach rose from 3.0% to 6.3% of dream sides, a clear move. Steak tartare and carpaccio hold 2.9% of starters.

05 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Lead the range with flavour cuts

    Short rib and ribeye are rising; fillet is slipping. Flavour cuts deliver the experience guests name at a better cost per plate.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNext quarter
  2. 02

    Supply a premium steak pie

    Steak pie rose from 0.7% to 3.1% of dream mains, a clear move. A named-beef pie for pubs and bistros.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumNext quarter
  3. 03

    Package braises for small kitchens

    Short rib is a braise: pre-cooked, portioned short rib lets small kitchens put it on without the labour.

    Impact: MediumEffort: MediumSix months
  4. 04

    Sell the plate, not just the cut

    Pair beef with greens and creamed spinach, the clear riser among sides. A costed steak-plate card for chefs.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowNext quarter
  5. 05

    Target the venues guests already name

    Start with Porter & Rye, The Spanish Butcher Glasgow and Gōst Bar & Restaurant, and the steakhouses that follow them.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowNow

06 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

Beef prices

If wholesale beef prices rise, the shift to braises and pies may speed up; a commission would pair this read with price data.

The beef group

Beef mains are up +5.3 points without a clear move yet (p = 0.086). One more strong quarter would confirm it.

Fillet

A continued fall would make fillet a special-occasion cut rather than a menu staple.

Plant-based mains

Vegetable sides are rising. Watch whether that reaches the main course and takes share from beef.

07 · Appendix

Method and sources

1,219dream mains
311beef and steak mains

6quarters tracked

Main and side shares use interviews with a codable answer for that course. "Steak (cut not named)" covers answers naming steak without a cut.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
