02 · The overlap

Favourites are likelier to be in the guides, but most are not

Guide coverage by guest popularity band.

So what for a tourism body

The guides and the guests agree more than chance would suggest (40% of the top 25 against 7% overall), but 79 of the hundred venues guests name most are not in the big guides. Add the Scottish, UK and bar awards we also track and 40 of the top 100 hold some recognition; 19 of them only outside the big guides. A visitor following only the big guides misses most of what locals love.

03 · Where they meet

Bib Gourmand is where guests and inspectors agree

The most-named venues, laid out by guide status.

5 of the 25 most-named venues hold a Bib Gourmand (Sebb's, Ka Pao, Margo, Ox and Finch, Ardfern). The starred venues guests name (LYLA, UNALOME by Graeme Cheevers, Cail Bruich, The Kitchin) sit between ranks 62 and 136: admired, but named less often than the city's casual favourites.

04 · The gap

The favourites the big guides miss

High guest advocacy, outside the guides we track.

So what for press and city marketing

Crabshakk Finnieston is the most-named venue in the archive and is not in the guides we track. A guest favourites list alongside the guides gives visitors and press the local view.

05 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Publish a guest favourites trail beside the guides

    79 of the top 100 named venues are outside the guides we track. A trail built from guest mentions fills the gap the big guides leave.

  2. 02

    Use Bib Gourmand as the bridge

    5 of the top 25 hold a Bib Gourmand: the award guests and inspectors agree on most. Lead visitor food campaigns with it.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNow
  3. 03

    Pitch the favourites beyond the big guides to press

    Start with Crabshakk Finnieston, Sugo Pasta, Big Counter: high guest advocacy, little big-guide coverage.

    When the 2027 guides publish, measure it again: a rising share means the guides are catching up with guests.

06 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

2027 guide editions

MICHELIN Great Britain and Ireland 2027 publishes early in 2027. New Scottish stars or Bibs among guest favourites would narrow the gap.

Visit frequency

Guests name places they go often; tasting-menu venues are visited less. A commission could weight by visit frequency.

Beyond Glasgow

Most named venues are in Glasgow. Targeted fieldwork elsewhere would test whether the pattern holds.

Award inflation

If guide selections expand, overlap rises without guests changing their minds. Track awards and mentions together.
