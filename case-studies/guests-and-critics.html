No. 08Dream Menu Insight Series · Reputation study

Guests and critics

Do the guides and the guests agree? This study sets the venues Dream Menu guests name most against MICHELIN, 50 Best, AA and Good Food Guide awards.

Written for
A city marketing or tourism body
The question

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

Guests and critics agree more than chance would suggest, especially on Bib Gourmand, but most guest favourites hold no guide award, so a visitor offer built only on the guides misses most of what locals love.

  • 40%of top 25

    Favourites are likelier to be recognised

  • 79of top 100

02 · The overlap

Favourites are likelier to be recognised, but most are not

Guide awards by guest popularity band.

So what for a tourism body

The guides and the guests agree more than chance would suggest (40% of the top 25 against 5% overall), but 79 of the hundred venues guests name most hold no guide award at all. A visitor following only the guides misses most of what locals love.

03 · Where they meet

Bib Gourmand is where guests and inspectors agree

The most-named venues, laid out by guide status.

5 of the 25 most-named venues hold a Bib Gourmand (Sebb's, Ka Pao, Margo, Ox and Finch, Ardfern). The starred venues guests name (LYLA, UNALOME by Graeme Cheevers, Cail Bruich, The Kitchin) sit between ranks 62 and 135: admired, but named less often than the city's casual favourites.

04 · The gap

The favourites the guides miss

High guest advocacy, no guide award.

So what for press and city marketing

Crabshakk Finnieston is the most-named venue in the archive and holds no guide award. A guest favourites list alongside the guides gives visitors and press the local view.

05 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Publish a guest favourites trail beside the guides

    79 of the top 100 named venues hold no guide award. A trail built from guest mentions fills the gap the guides leave.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumNext season
  2. 02

    Use Bib Gourmand as the bridge

    5 of the top 25 hold a Bib Gourmand: the award guests and inspectors agree on most. Lead visitor food campaigns with it.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNow
  3. 03

    Pitch the unrecognised favourites to press

    Start with Crabshakk Finnieston, Sugo Pasta, Big Counter: high guest advocacy, no guide coverage.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowNow
  4. 04

    Tell venues awards are not the only route

    Most guest favourites built their reputation without a guide award. Operator support should value word of mouth as much as inspection.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowOngoing
  5. 05

    Re-run the overlap each year

    When the 2027 guides publish, measure it again: a rising share means the guides are catching up with guests.

    Impact: LowEffort: LowAnnually

06 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

2027 guide editions

MICHELIN Great Britain and Ireland 2027 publishes early in 2027. New Scottish stars or Bibs among guest favourites would narrow the gap.

Visit frequency

Guests name places they go often; tasting-menu venues are visited less. A commission could weight by visit frequency.

Beyond Glasgow

Most named venues are in Glasgow. Targeted fieldwork elsewhere would test whether the pattern holds.

Award inflation

If guide selections expand, overlap rises without guests changing their minds. Track awards and mentions together.

Method and sources

Each venue guests name is matched to the Dream Menu awards list (MICHELIN 2026 editions, the 50 Best lists, AA Rosettes, the Good Food Guide and others; see the award winners page). "Any guide award" includes MICHELIN selected. Bands are by number of guest mentions.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
