01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

The next wave is real food with a story (ferments, retro classics, smaller protein-led plates, Asian dessert flavours) rather than another viral novelty, and it reaches mainstream Glasgow menus two to three years after it first appears.

  • 6crossing over

    Where to lead

    Ferments, Korean, retro, single-dessert shops, smaller portions and regional Mexican are moving from specialists to independents now.

  • 220"bin it" votes

    Hype burns out in a year

    Matcha and Dubai chocolate went from novelty to irritation inside two years.

  • 2 to 3years

    The window

    From first sighting to mainstream Glasgow menus, usually by way of London and Edinburgh.

What we recommend

  1. Range ferments and pickles now
  2. Retire Dubai chocolate, and limit pistachio and hot honey to one dish each
  3. Swap matcha for hojicha on the drinks list
  4. Build a winter retro menu
  5. Develop smaller-portion and protein lines
  6. Trial one Asian dessert flavour in summer 2027
  7. Partner a Scottish seaweed producer

02 · The curve

26 items on one adoption curve

Where each item sits today, and how sure we are.

So what for menu developers

The useful part of the curve is the middle. Fringe items are too early to bet on and items past the peak are already costing goodwill. The six items crossing over now are where a menu or a range can lead without gambling.

03 · The hype ledger

What guests want gone outweighs what they want more of

Set side by side, the two questions show what is burning out and what replaces it.

Two patterns repeat. First, a trend reaches the "bin it" list within about a year of its peak: matcha and Dubai chocolate went from novelty to irritation inside 2025 and 2026. Second, what guests ask for instead is rarely another novelty. Local and seasonal food, retro classics and ferments all point the same way: real food with a story, made properly. The exception is small plates, the one trend that is both loved and loathed, which means the format is right and the execution (portion, price, pacing) is what guests are judging.

04 · Arrivals

When the arrivals reach Glasgow

Expected timing for the items on the way up.

So what for suppliers

Most arrivals take two to three years from first sightings to mainstream in Glasgow, usually by way of London and then Edinburgh. That gap is the window to develop, test and range a product before demand peaks.

  • 01

    A chef names it before a guest does

    Items start in chef and staff answers. When guests start naming them unprompted, the item is crossing over.

  • 02

    It arrives as a flavour, then a dish

    Pandan and black sesame arrive in lattes and ice cream first. A dedicated dish is the sign of the next stage.

  • 03

    Edinburgh first, Glasgow a year later

    Stirred drinks and specialist formats tend to land in Edinburgh first; Glasgow adopts and scales.

  • 04

    The "bin it" list is the peak

    When an item first appears in "bin it" answers, it has peaked. Retire it within two seasons.

07 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

New "bin it" entries

The first "bin it" mention of an item on the way up means it has peaked faster than expected.

Guest-named arrivals

An item named by three or more guests in a quarter moves up a stage.

Hojicha against matcha

If matcha leaves the "bin it" list without hojicha rising, green tea fatigue is general, not matcha-specific.

Seaweed supply

Kelp crofting volumes and price decide whether seaweed can reach chain menus.

08 · Appendix

Method and sources

26items assessed
150trend answers coded
497"bin it" mentions (top 10)
5adoption stages

Stage, timing and confidence are Dream Menu judgement, combining what guests say (the "more of" and "bin it" questions), what they choose (six quarters of dream dishes and drinks) and published trade research. "Mainstream in Glasgow" means we would expect to find the item on menus in several chains or on most independent menus in a district. Confidence is high where guest data and outside research agree, medium where one leads, and low where the item is speculative. The "more of" question began on 13 August 2026; answers were coded by hand and counts are approximate.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.

Sources: Food Manufacture, August 2026, FoodNavigator, October 2026, Time Out, The Scotsman, SRUC kelpcrofting case study, The Caterer, Euronews, February 2025, Datassential via Food Manufacture, October 2026, Bidfood via The Grocer, Whole Foods Market, October 2026, IGD via Restaurant Online, July 2026.
