No. 11Dream Menu Insight Series · Sector pulse

The confidence gap

Scottish operators back their own businesses but not the sector around them, and almost none can see beyond the next quarter. A sector pulse from the 2026 Look Ahead survey.

Written for
A lender, local authority or trade body
The question
How are operators really doing, and what support would help most?
Status

38 of 52call the local sector mixed or deteriorating

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

Operators are confident in themselves but not in the sector, and almost none can see beyond a quarter, so the most useful support smooths a bad quarter: rates relief, bridging finance and footfall, aimed first at small businesses.

  • 6.5of 10

    They back themselves

    Average confidence in their own next three months; 30 of 54 score 6 or more.

  • 38of 52

    They doubt the sector

    Call their local sector mixed or deteriorating; only 5 say improving.

  • 3.0of 10

    Nobody can see ahead

    Predictability of the next quarter; nobody scores above 5.

What we recommend

  1. Target support at small businesses (11 to 50 staff)
  2. Offer bridging, not growth, finance
  3. Make rates relief the headline ask
  4. Fund footfall initiatives with events
  5. Run the pulse every quarter

02 · The confidence gap

Operators back their own business but not the sector

Trading, predictability and confidence, in full distribution.

So what for a lender or council

This is the confidence gap: operators average 6.5 for their own next quarter, yet 38 of 52 call their local sector mixed or deteriorating, and 22 of 54 are less confident about the year than the quarter.

03 · The pressure

Costs, not demand, are what hurts

The pressures operators name and the four-week trends they see.

04 · The response

Operators are acting; rates relief is the shared ask

What they have done, what would help, and who is feeling it most.

So what for policy makers

Small businesses (11 to 50 staff) rate trading at 3.8 against 5.8 for micro businesses: big enough to carry a full wage bill and rates, too small to absorb shocks. 44 of 55 name rates relief as the support that would help most.

05 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Target support at small businesses (11 to 50 staff)

    Their trading score (3.8) is the lowest of any size band with enough answers.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumNext budget round
  2. 02

    Offer bridging, not growth, finance

    Predictability averages 3.0 of 10. Products that smooth a bad quarter fit better than expansion loans.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumNext quarter
  3. 03

    Make rates relief the headline ask

    44 of 55 operators name it: the one measure that unites the sector.

    Impact: HighEffort: HighNext budget round
  4. 04

    Fund footfall initiatives with events

    Tourism and footfall initiatives are among the top three asks; events are named as a performance factor.

    Impact: MediumEffort: MediumSix months
  5. 05

    Run the pulse every quarter

    One survey is a snapshot. A quarterly pulse turns it into an early-warning system for lenders and councils.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowQuarterly

06 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

A second survey

A second pulse would show whether the gap is closing or widening. One survey is a snapshot.

Energy prices

Energy and food costs lead the pressures. A fall in energy prices would ease the biggest single cost line.

Closures

"More closures nearby" is the most-seen warning signal (25 of 55). Track closures by area.

Sample mix

Micro and small businesses are 48 of 55 answers: a fair picture of Scottish hospitality, thin for larger operators.

07 · Appendix

Method and sources

55operators
28micro businesses
20small businesses
5weeks of fieldwork

The 2026 Look Ahead survey ran 2 April to 3 May 2026. Respondents were owners, chefs, managers and suppliers across cafés, restaurants, hotels, bars, street food and catering. Scores are 1 to 10. Several questions allowed more than one answer. Figures here are recomputed from the anonymised scores, so they can differ by one or two from the 2026 report, which used an earlier extract.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
