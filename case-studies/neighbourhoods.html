Where Glasgow eats

Which Glasgow districts do guests talk about, which punch above their weight, and where is the next Finnieston? A location read built from every venue guests have named.

Written for
A property developer or landlord with food and drink units
The question
Which neighbourhoods should we lease to food and drink operators next?

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

Guest attention is concentrated between the Merchant City and Finnieston, but the Southside matches Finnieston on mentions per venue and has more of the hidden-gem answers: it is the district to lease into next.

  • 59%of mentions

    One line holds the conversation

    City centre, Finnieston and Merchant City hold 59% of mentions of mapped Glasgow venues.

  • 4.6per venue

    The Southside punches above its weight

    Against 3.4 in the city centre; only Finnieston is comparable.

  • 28%anchor share

    Destinations make districts

    Big Counter alone holds 28% of Southside mentions.

What we recommend

  1. Prioritise Southside units for food-led tenants
  2. Lead with one destination tenant per street
  3. Take early positions in the East End
  4. Use the city centre for brand-led concepts
  5. Track district momentum quarterly

High mentions per venue with a single strong anchor is the signature of a district on the way up. The Southside has both: 4.6 mentions per venue and Big Counter holding 28% of them.

05 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Prioritise Southside units for food-led tenants

    4.6 mentions per venue, one of the two highest in the city, with a high hidden-gem share: demand ahead of supply.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumNext 12 months
  2. 02

    Lead with one destination tenant per street

    Anchors carry a large share of their districts (Big Counter, Ka Pao). A destination venue lifts the units around it.

    Impact: HighEffort: HighLeasing strategy
  3. 03

    Take early positions in the East End

    The Barras area has a high share of hidden-gem answers. Pair units with the market and events while rents allow.

    Impact: MediumEffort: Medium12 to 24 months
  4. 04

    Use the city centre for brand-led concepts

    The centre has the most venues and mentions but fewer per venue (3.4): volume, diluted. It suits known brands.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowOngoing
  5. 05

    Track district momentum quarterly

    Mentions per venue and hidden-gem share by area, every quarter, so the next district shows before rents move.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowQuarterly

06 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

Rents and vacancy

Dream Menu measures demand, not rent. A commission would pair this read with agent data on rents and vacant units by street.

Anchor risk

Districts that depend on one venue are exposed if it closes or moves. Watch anchor share alongside mentions.

New openings

A new venue entering an area's top three within two quarters is the earliest sign of momentum.

214 venues without an address

Some guests name a venue without enough detail to place it (233 mentions). Address research would sharpen the read.

07 · Appendix

Method and sources

468Glasgow venues mapped
1,587mentions
8districts
214venues not placed

Venues are those guests named in Glasgow with an exact address or a named street or neighbourhood, inside the city. Each is assigned to the nearest of eleven district centre points within 1.6 km (the Southside includes Govanhill, Strathbungo and Shawlands; the East End includes the Barras and Dennistoun; Woodlands includes Charing Cross); the rest are "elsewhere". Boundaries are indicative. Hidden-gem share counts mentions from the "favourite hidden gem" and "next stop" questions.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
