The cost of a night out in Glasgow, 2026

What food, drink and transport cost on a Glasgow night out, how to budget for one, and what it means for Dunns Food & Drinks.

£74 a typical night, per head
£34 on drink
£30 on food
£10 getting there and back

Summary

A typical night out in Glasgow costs about £74 a head in October 2026: around £30 on food, £34 on drink and £10 getting there and back. Plan £40 for a low-key pub night and £150 or more for a celebration, and add 20% because most people overspend.

Drink is the biggest and most elastic line

A city-centre pint is £5.20 to £6.20 and a cocktail £10.50 to £13.50, so the number of cocktails decides the bill. Glasgow drink prices run about 10% below Edinburgh.

Food is the most controllable line

Two courses in a mid-range Glasgow restaurant cost £30 to £40 a head; a set or pre-theatre menu saves £10 or more.

Transport has got cheaper

Scotland's £2 bus cap and night buses make getting home as cheap as £2, while a solo taxi is £10 to £30. The real constraint is timing, not cost.

Diners are trading down, not staying in

Dream Menu's 2026 data shows 72% of operators with flat or falling spend per head, as guests skip dessert and the extra drink. Classics such as steak, seafood, margaritas and martinis still win the spend that remains.

For Dunns

The commercial prize is helping venues protect spend per head: dessert, cocktail and premium soft drink ranges, costed set-menu kits, and a quarterly Glasgow Night Out Index co-published with Dream Menu.

Market context

A night out costs around a third more than it did in 2022, and the pressure on Scottish venues is still building. Glasgow remains one of the better-value big cities in the UK, which matters for anyone pitching to, or marketing for, the city's hospitality trade.

  • Hospitality prices are up about 30% in four years. Compound food and drink inflation for the sector has topped 30% since mid-2022, and average restaurant spend per head has risen from £25.35 to £30.08 (NielsenIQ, 2026).
  • Scottish pubs face a rates shock. UKHospitality Scotland forecasts the average Scottish pub's business rates rising 77% in 2026/27, to £26,385 (UKHospitality Scotland via Cater). Expect some of this to reach menu and bar prices over the next 12 months.
  • Consumers are price-aware. UK adults spend an average of £3,776 a year on nights out in pubs, bars and clubs (Aqua via The Drinks Business, Oct 2025), yet price is the top worry for 44% of people planning a night out (Rekom via The Spirits Business).
  • Public policy is cutting the cost of getting home. Scotland has capped bus singles at £2 across the SPT area from 7 September 2026 and permanently scrapped ScotRail peak fares in September 2025. Transport is the one line of the budget that has got cheaper.

Guinness prices from The Drinks Business, March 2026. Drink in Glasgow runs roughly 10% below Edinburgh and 20% below London; food and taxis are broadly in line with the UK average outside London.

Glasgow's price advantage is a selling point for the city's venues, but it also caps how much of the cost stack they can pass on before losing their value reputation.

Food

Dinner on a Glasgow night out costs £18 to £45 a head for most people, with a late-night bite adding £8 to £12. Food is the most controllable line: it can be skipped, swapped for a set menu, or upgraded for a special occasion.

  • A meal at an inexpensive Glasgow restaurant averages £17.20, and a three-course meal for two at a mid-range place £60.71 (Numbeo via Expatica, June 2026).
  • A dinner for two at an Italian restaurant with wine and dessert comes in around £59 (Expatistan, Oct 2026).
  • Across the UK, the average restaurant main has risen from about £11 in 2022 to just under £12.50 (NielsenIQ, 2026).

Glasgow's value strength is the middle of the market: independent bistros, small-plates spots and curry houses where two courses still come in under £35. Fine dining is priced closer to Edinburgh.

Drink

A city-centre pint in Glasgow now sits at roughly £5.20 to £6.20, still below Edinburgh (about £6) and London (about £6.75). Drink is the single biggest and most variable line in any night-out budget, typically 40% to 65% of total spend.

  • UK average pint: £5.34 in April 2026, up 3.3% year on year, with premium lager at £5.94 and stout at £5.74 (Morning Advertiser, May 2026).
  • Glasgow is the second-cheapest UK city for Guinness at £4.93 a pint, against a UK average of £5.41 and £6.87 in London (The Drinks Business, March 2026).
  • Edinburgh averages around £6 a pint, level with Bath and Bristol (Finder, 2026).
  • Upward pressure continues: Heineken raised wholesale prices 2.7% from February 2026 and Diageo raised draught Guinness 5.2% from April 2026 (thinkmoney).

Transport

Getting out and home costs £4 to £25 a head in Glasgow, and the choice of how to get home is the biggest swing factor. A £2 night bus and a solo £20 taxi can sit either side of the same evening.

The insight for operators: the cheapest way home in Glasgow is now cheaper than one soft drink. The real constraint is not cost but timing: the Subway closes early and the last trains leave around midnight, so venues that want late spend from suburban and Edinburgh visitors are competing with the train timetable.

Modelled budgets

A typical Saturday night out in Glasgow costs about £75 a head, a low-key night about £40, and a celebration £150 or more. The three personas below are built from the price grids above.

How the typical night splits

  • Drink£34
  • Food£30
  • Transport£10

Drink 46%, food 41%, transport 13%. The biggest swing factors are the number of cocktails and whether the taxi home is shared.

  • The catch-up: two cans at home, four pints at about £5.20, a chippy, bus in and night bus home
  • The classic Saturday: two courses with service, five drinks mixing pints, wine and a cocktail, bus in and half a £16 to £20 taxi home
  • The big night: a pre-dinner cocktail, mid-range dinner with shared wine, four drinks across a cocktail bar and club, £10 entry, a late bite and a solo taxi

Benchmarks: UK survey work has put the average night out at £45 to £73 a head over recent years, depending on the definition and who was asked (thinkmoney, Rekom via The Spirits Business). Allowing for roughly 30% hospitality inflation since 2022, the £74 Glasgow model sits in the middle of that range.

How to budget for a night out

Budget £75 a head for a standard Glasgow night out, then add a 20% buffer: most people overspend once the night gets going. In one UK survey, 78% of 18 to 35 year olds said they routinely go over their planned budget (Confidentials).

Type of nightPlan forWith 20% buffer
Low-key pub night£40£48
Dinner and drinks£75£90
Celebration or club night£150£180

Costs people forget

  • Service charge of 10% to 12.5% on restaurant bills
  • Club entry of £5 to £15 and cloakroom of £2 to £3
  • Rounds: in a group of five, buying a round of cocktails costs £50 to £65 in one go
  • Surge pricing on Uber and private hire after 2am, often 1.5 to 2 times the normal fare
  • The Sunday trap: the Subway stops around 6pm, so Sunday nights default to taxis

Ways to spend less without missing out

  • Use the £2 bus cap both ways, or the £4.75 First 6pm Plus ticket for unlimited travel after 6pm
  • Eat early: pre-theatre and set menus save £10 to £15 a head against à la carte
  • Swap one cocktail for a pint or a premium soft drink and save £5 to £8
  • Split the taxi home; a shared £18 fare is £6 each between three
  • Drink in the Southside or a local pub rather than a city-centre cocktail bar, saving up to £2 a drink

What Dream Menu data adds

Glasgow's food-literate diners still go out, but they are trading down inside the bill: 72% of operators in the Dream Menu 2026 survey report spend per head flat or falling, while 87% report supplier prices rising. The interview data shows where the money still goes, and it lines up with the most expensive lines in this budget. Two datasets sit behind this section: 962 community interviews with chefs, hospitality staff, food writers and diners, and a 55-operator field survey from April 2026 (Dream Menu Hospitality Intelligence Report 2026). All mentions are unprompted.

  • Classic cocktails carry the premium end of the bill. Margaritas (66) and martinis (64) together outrank beer (116), and these are the £10 to £13.50 drinks in the price grid. A night built on two cocktails costs roughly double a night built on two pints.
  • Soft drinks are a real part of the round. Soft and diet drinks are the single most-named dream drink (143). Irn Bru beats Coca-Cola 435 to 307, and 21% chose neither. At £3.80 a time in town, no and low choices are no longer the cheap option.
  • Diners spend on classics, not gimmicks. Steak is the most-named main (251), seafood leads starters (303, with scallops alone at 99) and chips lead sides (240). Dubai chocolate (124 mentions) and matcha (90) top the list of trends people want gone, so premium pricing on novelty items is resisted.
  • The extras are what get cut. 42% of operators see lower spend per head locally, and operators describe guests skipping the starter, the second cocktail or the dessert. That is why the modelled budgets treat dessert and a final drink as optional lines.
  • Value price points work. One operator's £30 three-course lunch has taken off, and lunch vouchers kept another fully booked through January and February. A clear, honest set price is what converts a cautious diner.
  • The late-night end is comfort food. Pizza (141), kebab (36) and the chippy (23) dominate guilty pleasures, which is the £8 to £12 late-night bite line. A late-night dessert offer is one of the report's documented concept gaps.
  • Where the night happens is shifting. The Barras draws 50 mentions, more than any single venue, and the Southside has the widest gap between community advocacy and press attention, with rents well below Finnieston.

Food input costs top the stack, and drink input costs are named by only 26%. That gap is why menu prices are rising faster than bar prices, and it is the line a food distributor can most directly help with.

Figures in this section are from the Dream Menu Hospitality Intelligence Report 2026 (962 interviews and the April 2026 operator survey). The live site now holds 1,238 interviews; the report counts are kept as published.

Joint content idea: the Glasgow Night Out Index. A quarterly price check of a fixed basket (pint, glass of wine, cocktail, soft drink, casual main, late-night bite, taxi home), published in the Dunns-sponsored quarterly report alongside Dream Menu demand data. It fits the existing quarterly summary and sales-team presentation, gives the press a repeatable headline number, and positions Dunns as the distributor that understands what a night out costs the customer, not only the venue.
