The cost of a night out in Glasgow

What food, drink and transport cost on a Glasgow night out in 2026, how to budget for one, and where a food and drink distributor can help venues keep the spend that guests are cutting.

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

Glasgow is still good value at about £74 a head, but guests are trading down inside the bill, so the prize for a distributor is helping venues keep the dessert, the last drink and the soft round.

  • £74a head

    A typical night, split three ways

    About £30 food, £34 drink and £10 transport. A low-key night is £40 and a celebration £150 or more; add 20% because most people overspend.

  • 10%cheaper

    Good value caps pricing

    Drink runs about 10% below Edinburgh and 20% below London. The price advantage sells the city but limits what venues can pass on.

  • 72%of operators

    The extras are what get cut

    Spend per head is flat or falling as guests skip dessert and the extra drink. Classics still win the spend that remains.

What we recommend

  1. Lead sales calls with a "save the last spend" range
  2. Launch a "Glasgow classics" bar pack
  3. Co-publish a quarterly Glasgow Night Out Index
  4. Price soft drinks and no/low as margin lines
  5. Build costed £22 and £30 set-menu kits
  6. Offer one-quarter price holds on core lines
  7. Target new accounts in emerging districts

02 · The bill

A typical night costs £74 a head, and the extras decide it

Three modelled nights out, built from Glasgow prices: the catch-up, the classic Saturday and the big night.

What each night includes. The catch-up: two cans at home, four pints at about £5.20, a chippy, bus in and night bus home. The classic Saturday: two courses with service, five drinks mixing pints, wine and a cocktail, bus in and half a £16 to £20 taxi. The big night: a pre-dinner cocktail, dinner with shared wine, four drinks across a cocktail bar and club, £10 entry, a late bite and a solo taxi.

So what for Dunns

Drink and the optional extras decide the bill. That is where venues are losing spend, and where a supplier can help them keep it.

03 · Market context

A night out costs a third more than in 2022

Prices, rates and consumer worry are rising together; transport is the one line that has got cheaper.

A night out costs around a third more than it did in 2022. Glasgow remains one of the better-value big cities in the UK, which is a selling point for its venues but also caps how much of the cost stack they can pass on before losing their value reputation.

SignalWhat it means
Hospitality prices are up about 30% in four yearsAverage restaurant spend per head has risen from £25.35 to £30.08 (NielsenIQ, 2026).

04 · Food and drink

Drink is the biggest and most elastic line

Where the money goes behind the bar and on the plate.

A city-centre pint sits at £5.20 to £6.20, below Edinburgh (about £6) and London (about £6.75). The UK average pint was £5.34 in April 2026, up 3.3% on the year, and brewers raised wholesale prices again in 2026. Food is the most controllable line: dinner runs £18 to £45 a head, a meal at an inexpensive Glasgow restaurant averages £17.20, and a set or pre-theatre menu saves £10 or more.

Scottish factor

Minimum unit pricing (65p a unit in shops) lifts the cost of drinking at home, so the gap between pre-drinks and drinking out is narrower in Scotland than in England.

05 · Transport

Getting home is cheap; getting home late is hard

So what for venues

The cheapest way home is now cheaper than one soft drink. The constraint is timing: the Subway closes early and the last trains leave around midnight, so late spend from suburban and Edinburgh visitors competes with the timetable.

06 · What Dream Menu adds

Guests are trading down inside the bill, not staying in

What 962 interviews and the operator survey say about where the money still goes.

Two datasets sit behind this section: 962 community interviews and the operator survey from April 2026, as published in the Dream Menu Hospitality Intelligence Report 2026. All mentions are unprompted.

  • Classic cocktails carry the premium end of the bill. Margaritas (66) and martinis (64) together outrank beer (116), and these are the £10 to £13.50 drinks in the price grid.
  • No and low is no longer the cheap option. Irn Bru beats Coca-Cola 435 to 307, and at £3.80 a time in town a soft round costs real money.
  • Diners spend on classics, not gimmicks. Steak (251), seafood starters (303, scallops alone 99) and chips (240) lead; Dubai chocolate (124) and matcha (90) top the trends people want gone.
  • The extras are what get cut. 72% of operators report spend per head flat or falling; guests skip the starter, the second cocktail or the dessert.
  • The late-night end is comfort food. Pizza (141), kebab (36) and the chippy (23) dominate guilty pleasures; late-night dessert is a documented concept gap.

07 · Recommendations

What to do

Seven ideas for Dunns, each linking a cost-of-night-out finding to a product, a sales conversation or joint content. Start with the four quick wins in the first quarter.

  1. 01

    Lead sales calls with a "save the last spend" range

    Ready-to-plate desserts in the most-named formats (ice cream 221, sticky toffee pudding 122, tiramisu 120) and batch-friendly cocktail serves: the two lines guests cut first.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowQuarter 1
  2. 02

    Launch a "Glasgow classics" bar pack

    Tequila, gin, vermouth, citrus and premium mixers with a costed menu at a £10 to £11 sell price. Margarita 66 and martini 64 against beer 116.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowQuarter 1
  3. 03

    Co-publish a quarterly Glasgow Night Out Index

    A fixed basket (pint, wine, cocktail, soft drink, casual main, late bite, taxi home) in the Dunns-sponsored quarterly report: a repeatable headline for the press and the sales team.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumQuarter 1
  4. 04

    Price soft drinks and no/low as margin lines

    Premium soft drinks, Scottish mixers and alcohol-free pints with a menu template at £3.50 to £4.50. Soft drinks are the most-named dream drink.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowQuarter 1
  5. 05

    Build costed £22 and £30 set-menu kits

    Around the most-named dishes (steak 251, seafood starters 303, chips 240). Set price points are what operators say convert cautious diners.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumQuarter 2
  6. 06

    Offer one-quarter price holds on core lines

    87% of operators report supplier prices rising and 46% have changed or renegotiated suppliers in six months: a retention risk and an opening.

    Impact: MediumEffort: HighQuarter 2
  7. 07

    Target new accounts in emerging districts

    The Barras draws 50 mentions and the Southside has the widest gap between guest advocacy and press attention (see No. 07).

    Impact: MediumEffort: MediumQuarter 2

08 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

Rates and wage costs

If the 2026/27 rates rise reaches bar prices faster than expected, the night-out total moves above £80 and the set-menu kits become more urgent.

The £2 bus cap

The cap is why transport got cheaper. If it ends or narrows, the taxi line grows again and late spend suffers.

Spend per head

A second operator survey showing spend per head recovering would shift the focus from protecting spend to growing it.

Cocktail share

Cocktails are losing share of dream drinks (see No. 05). If that continues, weight the bar pack towards whisky and lager.

Glasgow price grids and personas are modelled, indicative figures, not a venue price survey. They start from published national and city benchmarks and apply typical Scottish venue-tier mark-ups. Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
