Ten forces to 2031

Weight-loss drugs, a generation drinking less, visitor levies and a menu turning to vegetables and fish: the ten forces that will reshape eating out in Scotland over the next five years, and what to do about each.

01 · Executive summary

The answer

In one sentence

The next five years will reward businesses that sell less but better: smaller portions, fewer and better drinks, vegetables, fish and named provenance, paid for in part by visitors through the new levies.

  • 4forces

    Plan for these now

    Rising costs, a generation drinking less, provenance and visitor levies are both high-impact and near-certain.

  • 3to prepare for

    Prepare scenarios

    How far weight-loss drugs spread, how durable the drinks shift is and how formats change are all big but uncertain.

  • 4already visible

    The data is ahead of the headlines

    Spirits, vegetables, white fish and Asian sides are already moving in Dream Menu answers.

What we recommend

  1. Plan for the four certain forces this year
  2. Write a smaller-appetite menu
  3. Rebuild the drinks list for fewer, better drinks
  4. Make provenance specific
  5. Retire one hype item a season
  6. Track the wildcards quarterly

02 · The ten forces

Ten forces will reshape eating out by 2031

Each with the number that matters, the evidence and when it bites.

01

The appetite shrinks

£1bnless out-of-home spend by 2031 from weight-loss drugs

IGD expects GLP-1 medicines to take almost £1bn out of eating-out spend by 2031 (IGD via Restaurant Online, July 2026). Users eat less and drink less; they still go out. Portions, protein and price per plate all change.

Bites: 2027 onwardShrinking
02

A generation drinks less

24%of adults did not drink in 2024, up from 19% in 2022

Fewer than 2% of people in their 20s drink daily, against 14% in their 60s (The Boar, October 2026). The bar has to make money from fewer, better drinks and a serious alcohol-free list.

Bites: NowShrinking
03

Drink less, drink better

+8.8 ptsspirits as a share of dream drinks, a clear move

Spirits rose from 4.8% to 13.6% of dream drinks and cocktails fell 18 points. Whisky and lager lead. Guests are moving from novelty to the classic pour.

Bites: NowReshaping
04

The cost stack keeps rising

4+licensed sites closing a day in Britain at the end of 2025

Wages (£12.71 from April 2026), rates (Scottish pubs up 77%) and energy keep rising; 382 sites closed in one quarter (NielsenIQ, January 2026). The middle of the market is the most exposed.

Bites: Now to 2029Shrinking
05

Cities tax visitors to fund themselves

£66ma year from the Edinburgh and Glasgow levies combined

Edinburgh's 5% levy went live in July 2026 (up to £50m a year); Glasgow's starts in January 2027 (about £16m) (City of Edinburgh Council, The Scotsman). Events and visitor spending will be planned around the money.

Bites: 2026 onwardGrowing
06

Vegetables move to the middle

+5.6 ptsvegetable and salad sides, a clear move

Vegetable sides rose and potato sides fell 11 points. Mediterranean sides are up too. This is guests choosing vegetables, not being told to.

Bites: NowGrowing
07

Provenance becomes the point

15of 150 guests want more local and seasonal food, the top answer

Asked which trend they want more of, guests name local, seasonal and Scottish produce more than anything else. White fish mains rose from 2.3% to 5.4%, a clear move.

Bites: Now to 2031Growing
08

Hype burns out faster

113guests want matcha gone; 105 want Dubai chocolate gone

Viral trends now reach the "bin it" list within a year of peaking. Novelty still sells for a season, but it no longer builds a reputation.

Bites: NowReshaping
09

Asia keeps arriving

+2.9 ptsAsian sides, a clear move

Korean, Japanese and South-East Asian flavours move from specialists to mainstream menus: hojicha, pandan, buldak and koji are all on 2027 trend lists (Datassential via Food Manufacture, October 2026).

Bites: 2027 to 2030Growing
10

The format gets rethought

11guests want more small plates, while 35 want them gone

Small plates are the only trend that is both loved and loathed. Set menus, solo-friendly portions, chef's counters and short menus are the formats that follow.

Bites: 2026 to 2029Reshaping

06 · Recommendations

What to do

Ranked by impact. The matrix shows where to start; the list says why.

  1. 01

    Plan for the four certain forces this year

    Costs, less drinking, provenance and visitor levies are high-impact and near-certain. Put each into the 2027 plan with an owner.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNow
  2. 02

    Write a smaller-appetite menu

    Half portions, protein-led dishes and price per plate protect covers as weight-loss drugs spread.

    Impact: HighEffort: MediumDuring 2027
  3. 03

    Rebuild the drinks list for fewer, better drinks

    Spirits up, cocktails down and young guests drinking less all point the same way.

    Impact: HighEffort: LowNext menu change
  4. 04

    Make provenance specific

    Local and seasonal is the most-wanted trend. Name the farm, the boat and the maker.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowNow
  5. 05

    Retire one hype item a season

    Hype burns out within a year. Keep novelty to a single, time-limited special.

    Impact: MediumEffort: LowEvery season

    Track the wildcards quarterly

    Weight-loss drug uptake and new formats are the least certain. Quarterly data shows which way they break.

07 · Risks and signals

What would change our view

The signals to track over the next two quarters, and what each would mean for the recommendations.

Weight-loss drug uptake

Faster uptake than IGD expects would move force 1 into "plan for it".

Alcohol-free share

If alcohol-free starts to gain share of dream drinks, force 2 has reached the menu.

Levy spending

Where the money goes decides whether force 5 lifts the whole year or only August.

Small plates

A clear rise in the "bin it" count would confirm the format shift in force 10.

How the projections work. Each line is a recency-weighted straight line through the quarters with enough answers, started from the latest solid quarter and damped by 30% each quarter so that no trend runs on for ever. The shaded band is a planning range that widens with the horizon and with the steepness of the trend, because a steep trend is the one most likely to stall. It shows how far the line could reasonably wander, not a statistical confidence interval. These are projections of the recent trend, not predictions: they say where things go if nothing changes, which is useful precisely because something usually does. Calls beyond 2027 are Dream Menu judgement, combining the projections with the published research cited.

Impact and certainty scores are Dream Menu judgement, made before reading the demand data so that the data could confirm or contradict them. Trend answers come from the question added on 13 August 2026 and are coded by hand.

Dish categories were made more specific on 24 October 2026; each answer is coded to the dish itself with a parent group. A clear move means a two-proportion test between July to December 2025 and April 2026 onward came out at p < 0.05. Dream Menu is qualitative fieldwork with food-literate guests, chefs and hospitality staff, not a statistically representative survey of the population. How Dream Menu works.
