The top 20 operator actions

From foundations (1 to 5: identity, menu, digital basics) through operational levers (6 to 15) to strategy (16 to 20).

  1. Define one clear, sayable reason to visit

    If a guest cannot describe you in one sentence, they are less likely to recommend you.

  2. Identify and protect your signature dish or drink

    Find what people already talk about most and treat it as a protected asset.

  3. Cut the bottom end of your menu

    Long menus signal uncertainty. Short ones signal confidence and support margin.

  4. Put one named supplier on the menu this week

    One real supplier with a real story says more than ten claims about quality.

  5. Update your online menu everywhere

    Website, Instagram link, Google and booking platforms should all show what you actually serve.

  6. Fix your Google Business profile

    Hours, menu link, current photos, an honest description. People on Google are close to deciding.

  7. Reply to your recent reviews properly

    Specific and warm. Never defensive, never silent.

  8. Replace AI or over-produced imagery with honest photos

    Real food, shot honestly, in the actual room.

  9. Give every server one genuine recommendation

    One real recommendation from someone who has eaten the dish beats a script.

  10. Design a stronger ending

    Dessert, the bill and the goodbye are disproportionately remembered.

  11. Build a drinks programme that matters

    Drinks are one of the clearest margin levers. Take them as seriously as the food.

  12. Create one accessible entry point

    A bar snack, a set lunch, a roast. Honest and easy to commit to.

  13. Design Sunday as a full proposition

    Its own menu, booking mechanics, provenance and atmosphere.

  14. Track RevPASH and real session performance

    Booking data, covers by session, dish sales, drinks mix. Discipline, not complexity.

  15. Stop carrying items that earn neither advocacy nor margin

    Every dish should build reputation, support margin, or both.

  16. Treat dessert as retained revenue

    A weak dessert hands the final spend and the final memory to someone else.

  17. Be explicit about the occasion you are built for

    Celebration, date, work lunch, treat, regular, walk-in, group.

  18. Invest in staff culture as a commercial system

    Retained people produce consistency and warmth guests notice.

  19. Choose your neighbourhood as carefully as your menu

    Site selection is a strategic skill, not just a property decision.

  20. Make big-event legacy count

    Keep the digital presence, group mechanics and visibility you build for events, and follow up first-time guests.

Menu copy and pricing

  • Name suppliers: "Greenheart Growers asparagus", not "seasonal asparagus".
  • Describe technique, not adjectives: "slow-braised", "house-fermented", not "tender" or "delicious".
  • Name dishes plainly so they are quotable.
  • Allergens: clear and confident, without a defensive preamble.
  • One premium anchor makes the rest of the menu feel fair.
  • One signature side or snack priced fairly can earn its own following.
  • A happy hour builds community and signals confidence.

Social media and reviews

The three-post week

  1. Monday or Tuesday, the dish: one honest photo in natural light, a short caption naming the dish and supplier.
  2. Thursday, the process or the place: prep, a supplier visit, the room. More intimate, first person.
  3. Weekend, the community: credit a guest's post, a staff recommendation, what's on this weekend.

Reels: one beautiful moment in 10 to 20 seconds, best frame first. TikTok: the process and the person, one take on a phone beats a produced video.

Content creators

The most valuable posts come from people who paid. "I went with my own money and it was the best thing I've eaten this year" is trusted far more than a gifted post. Make a venue worth posting about, then treat creators like any other guest.
