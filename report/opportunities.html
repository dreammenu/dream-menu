The seven dining occasions

People do not just go out to eat. They go for a birthday, a first date or a Tuesday when they cannot face cooking. Each is a different commercial job. Venues built for one occasion well outperform venues built for all of them equally.

Coffee, bakeries and sweet treats

74 respondents named a café, bakery, dessert or gelato venue as their next stop. Specialists win moments restaurants cannot: the mid-morning coffee, the post-dinner gelato walk. Own one item completely, treat gluten-free as a market rather than a constraint, and price for regulars.

Breakfast and brunch

Desire without a destination. People want craft applied to formats they love: a proper hollandaise, seasonal French toast, a full Scottish that earns the name. Not avocado toast.

The market-to-permanent pathway

The least risky route to a Glasgow opening now runs through a market or pop-up. Fook Mei spent two years at the Barras and opened its permanent site into an audience that was already queuing.

Five investable theses

Glasgow is undervalued against its performance. It is the fourth-largest UK catchment, national operators rank Glasgow sites in the top 10% of their portfolios, and prime rents sit well below London and Edinburgh.

  1. Back the next quality independent group

    The Scoop template: distinct venues, shared infrastructure, a long talent pipeline. A five to seven-year horizon.

  2. Late-night dessert

    The highest-confidence, lowest-capital gap: an estimated £150k to £250k capex for 30 to 40 covers, with a two to three-year first-mover window.

  3. District formation: the Barras

    At the turn from emerging to arrived. Mixed use with quality food and drink below, as Finnieston was in 2012.

  4. Hotel and independent partnerships

    The Celentano's and Arthouse model: the hotel brings the space and footfall, the concept brings identity.

  5. Quality brunch

    The most underprovided occasion relative to demand, with weekly repeat custom and low acquisition cost.

Trend forecasts

Now · 2026
  • Fire and smoke as a baseline technique
  • Scottish ingredients with global technique
  • Sri Lankan, Malaysian and Korean expanding
  • Artisan bread as a menu statement
  • Butterscotch replacing salted caramel
  • Brown butter as a hero flavour
  • Counter seating standard in new venues
  • Supper clubs growing
  • Named-supplier menus now expected
  • No/low as a serious category
  • Hugo Spritz replacing Aperol
  • TikTok the main discovery route for under-30s
2027 to 2030
  • West African as the next major wave
  • Fermentation from niche to standard
  • Whole-animal butchery returning
  • Non-alcoholic pairings standard
  • A brunch quality arms race
  • Late-night dessert concepts arriving
  • Neighbourhood bistros over city centre
  • Sunday roast destination venues
  • Dynamic pricing at the premium end
  • Booking data as a competitive tool
  • Staff wellbeing as a recruitment need
  • Indonesian and Filipino emerging
2031 and beyond
  • Older, affluent diners become the fastest-growing segment: earlier sittings, accessibility, allergen precision.
  • Climate and supply: direct grower relationships become an advantage that is hard to copy.
  • Alcohol in structural decline: no/low becomes a primary drinks category.
  • Food as Glasgow's identity, the way its music is.
  • Data-led operations pull away from instinct-led ones.
