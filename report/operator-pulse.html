The operator pulse

Fifty-four Scottish operators across cafés, restaurants, hotels, bars, street food and catering, surveyed 2 April to 3 May 2026. 87% are micro or small businesses, which is a fair picture of Scottish hospitality.

Visibility is the weakest number

Half the operators cannot meaningfully forecast the quarter ahead: 26 of 52 scored predictability 3, and nobody scored it 6 or above. Confidence fades with distance, from 6.5 at three months to 6.2 at twelve.

A K-shaped sector

41% are trading worse than a year ago, 30% better. Operators trading better hold twelve-month confidence of 7.4; those trading worse, 4.6. Luxury, events and differentiated concepts are pulling away. The middle-market independent is where the pain sits.

Scale is a hedge

The three large operators report twelve-month confidence of 7.7, against 6.5 for micro and 5.5 for small businesses. Restaurants average 4.2 for trading conditions; events and catering 7.3.

The four-line cost stack

Share naming each as a most significant pressure.

  • Food input costs63%
  • Energy / utilities61%
  • Labour costs57%
  • Rent / rates / property52%
  • Consumer demand / footfall44%
  • Debt / cashflow / financing33%
  • Drink input costs26%
  • Weather / seasonality20%
  • Recruitment / retention15%
  • Supply chain reliability15%

46% name at least three of the four core cost lines. Only four operators name none.

What operators have done

Last 3 to 6 months.

  • Price changes61%
  • Marketing / partnerships / events50%
  • Supplier changes / renegotiation48%
  • Menu engineering / fewer items43%
  • Staffing model changes35%
  • Equipment / efficiency33%
  • Reduced opening hours22%
  • Changed concept or format20%
  • Sought additional finance15%
  • Renegotiated rent / lease7%

Rent renegotiation is the least-used lever (7%) against one of the biggest pressures (52%).

What actually made a difference

  • Revenue levers: menu engineering to a clear price point, such as a £30 three-course lunch that "has taken off", and lunch vouchers that kept January and February full.
  • Demand and marketing: social media pushes, TikTok, and "creating a sales team within my existing team".
  • Cost discipline: supplier renegotiation and equipment that cut waiting times.
  • Differentiation: "creating a unique offering unavailable elsewhere".

Less successful: shorter opening hours, and smaller menus that some guests missed.

Early-warning signals

  • More closures nearby44%
  • Lower spend per head43%
  • Fewer bookings / less footfall35%
  • More discounting needed19%
  • Supplier instability19%
  • More cancellations / no-shows11%
  • Higher staff churn7%

Spend per head is flat for 56% and down for 19%. With costs rising for 89%, flat spend is a real-terms fall. Guests are trading down, not out: skipping the starter, the second cocktail, the dessert.

What would help most

  • Tax / rates relief80%
  • Tourism / footfall initiatives39%
  • Energy support39%
  • Access to finance26%
  • Regulatory simplification19%
  • Local authority engagement19%
  • Skills / training9%

"VAT" appears unprompted in 11 open answers, 20% of the sample. Nothing else comes close.

For operators

  • Audit food, energy, labour and rent as one stack, not four separate lines.
  • Pull the rent and rates lever: a lease conversation or rates appeal is more likely to work than most assume.
  • Invest where the evidence points: a clear price point, targeted demand generation, a differentiated offer.
  • Watch spend per head more closely than covers.
  • Plan on rolling 13-week forecasts and flexible rotas while predictability is this low.

For decision makers

  • VAT relief in line with European hospitality norms is the ask the sector has organised around.
  • Structural business rates reform, not a one-off relief.
  • Energy support for hospitality SMEs.
  • Tourism and footfall initiatives that activate Scotland's existing advantage.
  • A single point of engagement for the sector.

Source: Dream Menu Hospitality Intelligence Report 2026, edition 6. Community data to 9 October 2026 (1,175 interviews); operator survey April to May 2026 (54 operators). Live figures in the partner area include newer interviews, so some numbers differ slightly.
